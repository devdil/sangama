# Device simulation, 2026-09-29

First run of `scripts/simulate-fleet.py` with [`fleet-mixed.json`](../simulation/fleet-mixed.json): four simulated home devices, two per shard of Qwen2.5-0.5B. Each device ran in its own container on its own isolated network, so it could reach the others only through the relay. Each was shaped by its [profile](../simulation/device-profiles.json) (memory, CPUs, delay, jitter, bandwidth, loss, extra compute time) and slept and woke on its schedule. The profile values are assumptions, not measurements.

| Device | Profile | Shard |
|---|---|---|
| desk | desktop-fibre | 0 |
| phone | phone-4g | 0 |
| mac | macbook-wifi | 1 |
| oldlaptop | old-laptop-wifi | 1 |

## Results (180 s of continuous generation)

- Attempts: 18; succeeded: 16, all with the exact reference tokens (16).
- Failures: 1 mid-generation (a device slept during the request), 1 at planning (no ready device for shard 1).
- Sleep events: 6. Median first token: 1334 ms.
| Route | Generations | Median decode (tok/s) |
|---|---|---|
| desk -> mac | 14 | 6.665 |
| desk -> oldlaptop | 2 | 4.365 |

## Observations

- When the MacBook slept, the client re-planned onto the old laptop without intervention; speed dropped from 6.7 to 4.4 tok/s.
- A request in progress when its device slept failed; the next request re-routed. There is no mid-request failover, since KV-cache state lives on the sleeping device.
- At t=133 s both shard-1 devices were unavailable (one asleep, the other woken 0.7 s earlier and not yet reconnected), so planning failed. Two copies of a layer were not enough for devices that sleep this often; real fleets need two to three.
- The phone was never chosen, because the planner picks the fastest ready route.

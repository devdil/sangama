A small model across CPUs, GPUs and the Internet

29 September 2026

Measured on 29 September 2026 with the Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct test model, split across two workers. The full list of test scenarios and raw results is in the repository.

  • Same answer everywhere. At full precision, every combination produced exactly the same tokens: CPU, Apple Metal, NVIDIA CUDA and Vulkan, with either of the two engines, mixed in any order.
  • One machine, two GPUs: 103 tokens per second (220 with 4-bit weights).
  • Across the Internet: 3.1 tokens per second between a Mac on a home network and a GPU in a US datacenter, 260-370 ms apart. Distance, not computing power, sets the pace.
  • Through a relay: 7 out of 7 generations were exact over four minutes of continuous use, with every connection forced through the relay.

Speeds are single runs of a small test model and include network delay; they are not benchmarks. 4-bit output is only reproducible with the same weight file on the same kind of GPU, so a route must use one published file.

What the testing caught

  • GPU context bug: NVIDIA CUDA workers failed on every conversation after the first. Found, fixed, and re-tested over five conversations in a row.
  • Relay time limit: relay connections closed after two minutes, cutting off requests in progress. Raised to one hour; four minutes of continuous traffic then ran cleanly.
  • Mixed weight files: two differently compressed copies of the same model were accepted in one route. Workers now report a checksum, and such routes are refused.
